# Bitcoin FilmFest festival-history research brief

**Updated:** 2026-08-31
**Status:** active Phase 2 research; public-safe working brief

This is the starting brief for turning the private Notion extraction, public archive links, and owner knowledge into the strongest public Bitcoin FilmFest history. It is not a raw Notion export and must not become one.

## Editorial objective

Show how Bitcoin FilmFest developed from a Warsaw festival into a wider Bitcoin Cinema network: a festival, a travelling Cinema Room, compact Minis, conference collaborations, filmmaker conversations, awards, experiments, and human encounters.

The public result should be stories and useful archive pages, not an unverified schedule dump.

## Award framework from owner brain-dump

The award history should be reconstructed edition by edition with a dedicated Golden Rabbits archive.

- **Best Movie** — category used across the award history; winners still need to be identified for BFF’23–BFF’25.
- **Best Short** — not awarded/used at BFF’23; winner history from BFF’24 onward needs extraction.
- **Best Story** — category used at BFF’24 and BFF’25; winners need extraction.
- **Audience Choice** — category used in the award history; winners need extraction.
- **BFF’26 confirmed from the post-festival source:** *Bitcoin Castle* won Best Movie and Audience Choice; *MaxisClub* won Best Short; Best Story was not awarded.

Do not infer winners from nominees, programme pages, or press mentions. A missing winner becomes an owner question.

## Story candidates

### 1. The cinema that travels

Warsaw is the anchor, not the limit. Lugano provides the clearest format: an ambient Cinema Room where people could drop in for short works, plus a focused half-day of screenings and conversations. This can introduce Minis as a flexible cultural format rather than a smaller copy of the main festival.

### 2. From one festival to a network

Build a visual/text timeline from BFF’23 through BFF’26, then branch outward to Lisbon, Lugano, Madeira, Cape Town, Bitfest and Maribor. The point is not the number of places; it is the change in how Bitcoin Cinema reaches communities.

### 3. Films as a gateway

Use owner anecdotes about newcomers, local Bitcoin communities and films as education/cultural bridges. Connect this to African adoption stories, community documentaries, short films and creator conversations.

### 4. The Golden Rabbit archive

Create an award timeline with category availability by year, winner, film, creator, image of the award where permitted, and a short note on why that work mattered. BFF’26 is the first fully confirmed node; earlier editions need owner/archive research.

### 5. BFF’26: culture in convergence

The post-festival material supports a rich recap: films, AI contest, art, privacy masks, MoneroKon cross-attendance, creator panels, international guests, volunteers, newcomers, and the Golden Rabbits. This should read as a cultural portrait, not a statistics page.

Use clearly marked temporary generated placeholders in the next design iteration so the page structure can be tested. Replace them later with owner-supplied images.

Potential slots:

Before replacing placeholders, record edition, photographer/creator if known, permission status, and the intended alt text. Do not publish private folder links or assume that an attendee photo is cleared merely because it exists in the archive.

## Film expansion rule

The next catalogue scan should include every title that meets one or more of these discovery signals:

- screened at a prior BFF edition or Mini/roadshow;
- named in a BFF press release or public edition page;
- present in the curated Notion programme/archive material;
- directly connected to a BFF award, creator, panel or public story.

That is a **discovery rule**, not automatic publication. Each candidate still needs a public-safe synopsis, Bitcoin angle, source URLs, stable identity, status, and a map entry showing why it is included. Prioritize the next 10–20 films by richness and BFF relevance, not only by database order.

## Standing questions for future iterations

Every archive or film iteration must end with a short owner question set. The default categories are:

1. attendance and counting method;
2. award categories and winners;
3. exact event dates, names and venues;
4. guests, filmmakers and memorable moments;
5. photos, videos, posters, programmes and permissions;
6. films that the owner considers essential or misrepresented by the database.

The owner’s answers are editorial source material and should be recorded with date and scope, then reflected in the private ledger and the public copy where appropriate.
